Overview

Protect your people, your license and your business

California requires every employer to carry workers' compensation insurance, even with a single part-time employee. It pays medical care and lost wages when an employee is hurt on the job, and in exchange generally protects you from being sued by that employee.

Getting the classification codes and payroll right is where most businesses overpay. We review how your work is classified, compare multiple carriers, and set you up so your year-end payroll audit doesn't come with a surprise bill.

Operating without coverage is a misdemeanor in California and can lead to stop orders and penalties from the state, on top of paying claims out of pocket.

What it covers

Coverage built around your business

Medical care

Doctor visits, hospital stays, therapy and prescriptions for work-related injuries and illnesses.

Lost wages

Temporary and permanent disability benefits while an employee recovers.

Employer's liability

Defense and coverage if you are sued in connection with a workplace injury.

Return-to-work support

Programs that help injured employees get back to work sooner.

Pay-as-you-go options

Premiums based on actual payroll each pay period, so you avoid big audit bills.

CSLB filings

We file your certificate with the Contractors State License Board for you.

Questions

Workers' Compensation FAQ

Do I need workers' comp if I only have one employee?

Yes. California requires all employers to carry workers' compensation, even for one part-time employee.

How is workers' comp priced?

Premium is based on your payroll, the classification code for each type of work, and your claims history (experience modification). Getting codes right is one of the biggest ways we save clients money.

Can I get pay-as-you-go workers' comp?

Yes. Many of our carriers offer pay-as-you-go billing tied to your payroll, which reduces the upfront deposit and year-end audit surprises.

Do sole owners need workers' comp?

Owners with no employees usually aren't required to carry it, though contractors must file an exemption with the CSLB, and roofers (C-39) must carry it regardless.
