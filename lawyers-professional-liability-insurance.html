Overview

Protection for the practice you built

Lawyers professional liability (legal malpractice) insurance covers claims that an error, omission or missed deadline harmed a client, including defense costs, which can be substantial even when a claim has no merit.

California's Rules of Professional Conduct require attorneys without professional liability insurance to disclose that in writing to clients in many situations. Carrying coverage avoids the disclosure and protects your practice.

What it covers

Coverage built around your business

Errors & omissions

Claims alleging mistakes, missed deadlines or negligent advice.

Defense costs

Attorney fees and costs to defend covered claims.

Prior acts

Coverage for work done before the policy started, when available.

Disciplinary proceedings

Some policies include coverage for State Bar proceedings.

Questions

Lawyers Professional Liability FAQ

Do California lawyers have to carry malpractice insurance?

It is not mandatory, but attorneys without it generally must disclose that to clients in writing under California Rule of Professional Conduct 1.4.2.

Is legal malpractice insurance claims-made?

Yes, nearly all lawyers policies are claims-made, so keeping continuous coverage and prior acts dates matters.
